# Domain 6: Care Economy, Mental Health & Human Development

## Research Summary (October 2026)

- WHO estimates more than **1 billion people** live with a mental health condition. Depression and anxiety cost roughly **US$1 trillion a year** in lost productivity. Only about **9% of people with major depressive disorder receive minimally adequate treatment** globally; in sub-Saharan Africa the figure is about **2%**.
- The Alzheimer's Association's 2026 data estimate about **7 million Americans live with Alzheimer's**, supported by **12 million unpaid caregivers**; US dementia cost is about **US$409 billion in 2026**. RAND estimates more than **100 million US adults** provide unpaid care to an adult relative or friend.
- Care work is disproportionately female, unpaid or underpaid, and invisible in GDP. The burden is not only time; it is medication management, financial navigation, isolation and the absence of a break.
- Youth self-harm, men's suicide, overdose grief, foster-care transitions and childcare quality are measurable seams where a small team can build a protocol, a peer network or a navigation layer.
- The 12 problems below are not "mental health apps". They are **actualising conditions** for care: respite, medication reconciliation, social connection, escalation, repair, credentials, grief support and supervision.

## Axioms and Aristotelian Frame

**Material cause:** human time, attention, bodies, medication, housing, money, and the physiology of stress and grief.

**Formal cause:** care contracts, benefit rules, school protocols, professional credentials, and the assumption that families will absorb whatever the system does not fund.

**Efficient cause:** family caregivers, home-care workers, teachers, peer groups, disability technicians, foster-care navigators, and community mental health workers.

**Final cause:** people are cared for without destroying the carer; children are safe; men and boys can ask for help; disabled people can move; grief is witnessed; and paid care work is dignified.

**Axiom list:**

1. Care is continuous; a break must be designed, not assumed.
2. Polypharmacy and fragmented records cause preventable harm.
3. Loneliness is a health risk, not a mood.
4. Self-harm is a signal that requires a pathway, not punishment.
5. Men seek help less often because services are designed around women's help-seeking patterns.
6. A broken assistive device is a loss of mobility, income and dignity.
7. Care work becomes safer and more reliable when it has a portable credential and supervision.
8. Grief and caregiving are social experiences; isolation is the comorbidity.

**Derivation:** A1 → micro-respite. A2 → medication reconciliation. A3 → social prescribing. A4 → school escalation protocol. A5 → men's peer settings. A6 → wheelchair repair network. A7 → home-care micro-credential and supervision. A8 → grief support.

## Portfolio Logic

The portfolio covers the care lifecycle: **relief** (rows 1, 8), **safety** (rows 2, 4, 10), **connection** (rows 3, 5), **mobility and quality** (rows 6, 9), **dignified work** (rows 7, 12), and **money** (row 11). It deliberately includes non-software services because the binding constraint is often a human relationship, not an app.

What is excluded: another generic therapy chatbot, another caregiver "content" app, another childcare marketplace, and another employee-assistance programme that nobody uses. Those are crowded or fail the trust test.

## Kill Criteria and Review Notes

- Every row has a measurable human outcome: stress, safety, connection, mobility, retention, housing, benefits recovered.
- The hard dependencies are safeguarding (rows 1, 4, 8, 10), clinical governance (rows 2, 4, 8, 12), employer or agency partners (rows 1, 7, 9, 11), and disability networks (row 6).
- The "who pays" answer is the employer, insurer, municipality, foundation or family that already bears the cost of breakdown.
- The ethics guardrail: no surveillance, no forced participation, no shaming, no religious coercion, no predatory finance, and no deployment without crisis escalation.
