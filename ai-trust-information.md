# Domain 5: AI Safety, Digital Trust & Information Integrity

## Research Summary (October 2026)

- The WEF *Global Risks Report 2026* ranks misinformation and disinformation among the top short-term risks and adverse AI outcomes among the fastest-rising long-term risks. The problem is no longer "can a model write text"; it is **agency, provenance, liability and trust**.
- Agentic AI moved into production faster than governance. 2026 surveys show most large enterprises running at least one agent workflow; security incidents and cost overruns are common. A 2026 SailPoint survey found **23% of organisations reported agents being tricked into revealing access credentials**.
- Documented deepfake fraud losses reached about **US$3.7 billion** by mid-2026, with estimates of **US$40 billion a year** in synthetic-media-enabled fraud. Voice and video cloning now account for roughly two-thirds of observed synthetic fraud cases.
- Small businesses and households are the least protected: they use agents, buy AI tools, and receive synthetic calls, but have no security team, no procurement leverage, and no easy verification.
- The 12 problems below are not "AI ethics" in the abstract. They are concrete trust mechanisms: scoped permissions, provenance, mandates, audits, and human confirmation at the moment of risk.

## Axioms and Aristotelian Frame

**Material cause:** tokens, permissions, signatures, payment rails, identity claims, and the human attention that decides whether to trust.

**Formal cause:** OAuth scopes, procurement contracts, payment mandates, evidence rules, platform terms, and the absence of liability assignment.

**Efficient cause:** IT service providers, accountants, banks, newsroom editors, court clerks, parents, and small teams who can build a verification layer.

**Final cause:** people and institutions can act online without being impersonated, drained, deceived or locked out; agents can be useful without being all-powerful.

**Axiom list:**

1. An agent inherits the permissions of its principal; broad permissions create a broad blast radius.
2. Untrusted content must never be treated as trusted instruction.
3. Voice, image and video are no longer proof of identity; shared secrets and out-of-band verification are.
4. Provenance is metadata plus signature plus a visible claim; without the visible part, it does not change trust.
5. Insurance and procurement need incident data; without it, risk is priced by fear.
6. A payment mandate must be scoped, signed and auditable.
7. Safety is testable; an audit that cannot fail is marketing.
8. Digital assets outlive their owners; access must be planned before death or incapacity.

**Derivation:** A1 → least-privilege audit for SMBs. A2 → tool-boundary policy for browser agents. A3 → family verification code. A4 → local-news provenance label. A5 → incident data for underwriting. A6 → mandate verifier. A7 → teen companion audit. A8 → digital legacy executor.

## Portfolio Logic

The portfolio moves from **permission** (rows 1, 6), to **content** (rows 2, 4, 8), to **identity and money** (rows 3, 11), to **institutions** (rows 5, 9, 12), to **time** (row 10). Each row is a trust mechanism that can be tested in under a year, and each has a payer who bears the cost of the failure: the MSP, the bank, the insurer, the newsroom, the school, the court.

What is excluded: another generic deepfake detector, another AI content moderation API, another chatbot safety wrapper, and another "AI governance" dashboard. Those are crowded or too abstract to adopt.

## Kill Criteria and Review Notes

- Every row has a measurable trust outcome: scopes removed, injections blocked, scams prevented, claims paid, stories labelled, cases accepted.
- The hard dependencies are platform APIs (rows 1, 2, 6), bank/PSP partnerships (rows 3, 11), insurer/broker partners (row 5), and legal/forensic review (rows 10, 12).
- The "who pays" answer is the institution that already loses money to the failure: MSP, bank, insurer, newsroom, school, court.
- The ethics guardrail: no surveillance of teens or elders, no credential backdoors, no partisan forensics, no pay-to-rank trust badges, and no fear marketing beyond the documented risk.
