# Domain 10: Industry, Semiconductors, Space & Defence

## Research Summary (October 2026)

- The counter-drone market is estimated at **US$14.4 billion in 2026**, growing about 22% a year. The US Congressional Budget Office estimates **US$74 million to equip one military installation** with layered counter-small-UAS defence and **US$5 million a year** to sustain it. Cheap drones have inverted the cost curve; detection, attribution and low-cost defeat matter more than exquisite interceptors.
- IEA's *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026* warns that export controls have turned concentration risk into an immediate economic-security problem. China dominates refining of rare earths and a large share of gallium, germanium, graphite and battery materials. Up to **US$6.5 trillion a year of downstream production** outside China could be affected by full rare-earth controls.
- Advanced manufacturing remains a chokepoint: semiconductor test, packaging, thermal interfaces and radiation-hardened compute are capital-intensive and concentrated. A 1–3 person team cannot build a fab, but it can build a **test, metrology or compliance layer** that makes existing capacity more reliable.
- Space weather can disrupt grids, aviation and GNSS. Most alerts are scientific rather than operational; utilities and airlines need a translated, local risk signal.
- Defence procurement and ammunition logistics are document-heavy, manual and safety-critical. The opportunity is not another weapon; it is **trust, traceability and readiness**.
- The 12 problems below are dual-use and export-control sensitive. Every one requires legal review, a compliance plan and a partner with the appropriate licence. They are included because the 2026 threat picture makes these seams urgent, not because they are easy.

## Axioms and Aristotelian Frame

**Material cause:** metals, semiconductors, propellant, batteries, heat, radiation, and the physical failure modes of complex systems.

**Formal cause:** export controls, procurement rules, certification, chain-of-custody, maintenance schedules, and the separation of design from test.

**Efficient cause:** test engineers, procurement officers, logistics units, space startups, MSME manufacturers, and quality inspectors.

**Final cause:** reliable equipment, safe soldiers and workers, trustworthy supply chains, and a defence posture that does not depend on a single foreign chokepoint.

**Axiom list:**

1. Complex systems fail at interfaces: packaging, connectors, counterfeit parts, thermal paths.
2. A part is only trustworthy if its provenance and test history are verifiable.
3. Ammunition and batteries degrade with time, temperature and handling; item-level data prevents waste and failure.
4. Procurement delay is a readiness tax; compliance can be automated.
5. Radiation and space weather are physical threats that can be modelled and mitigated.
6. Counterfeit and substandard components enter through the least-visible tier of the supply chain.
7. Heat is a measurable cause of soldier and worker degradation.
8. Export controls and certification are not paperwork; they are part of the product.

**Derivation:** A1 → test fixtures, thermal interfaces, robot safety. A2 → component authenticity. A3 → ammunition lifecycle monitoring. A4 → procurement compliance automation. A5 → space weather alerts and rad-hard compute. A6 → counterfeit detection. A7 + A8 → soldier heat/power systems and compliance.

## Portfolio Logic

The portfolio is a **reliability and trust layer** for industry and defence: verify parts (rows 1, 10), track consumables (rows 2, 4), automate compliance (row 3), make automation safe (row 5), test and package chips (rows 6, 7), anticipate space weather (row 8), harden compute (row 9), and protect the human (rows 11, 12).

What is excluded: another drone manufacturer, another counter-drone interceptor, another defence chatbot, and another "smart factory" dashboard. Those are crowded, capital-intensive, or fail the 1–3 person test.

## Kill Criteria and Review Notes

- Every row requires a compliance and export-control review before any pilot. No exceptions.
- The hard dependencies are defence/space partners (rows 1, 2, 3, 9, 11, 12), test labs (rows 4, 6, 7, 10), and utilities/aviation (row 8).
- The "who pays" answer is the prime contractor, depot, test lab, insurer or mission programme that bears the failure cost.
- The ethics guardrail: no weapons targeting, no surveillance of soldiers beyond health, no export-control violations, no overstated security claims, and human sign-off on every automated decision.
