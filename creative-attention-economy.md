# Domain 12: Creative, Cultural & Attention Economy

## Research Summary (October 2026)

- Generative AI has made voice, image, music and video cheap to produce. The scarce assets are now **consent, provenance, taste, cultural context and rights**.
- India's media and entertainment sector is large and growing, but creators remain underpaid and under-protected. Voice actors, translators, comedians, artisans and animators face opaque contracts, unauthorised model training and weak attribution.
- Cultural heritage is degrading physically while digitisation remains expensive and inaccessible to small archives and community museums.
- The 12 problems below are **rights and infrastructure** plays, not another content generator. They help creators prove ownership, license work, get paid and preserve culture.

## Axioms and Aristotelian Frame

**Material cause:** voice, sound, images, performances, craft objects, film, and the attention of audiences.

**Formal cause:** copyright contracts, licensing terms, platform rules, attribution norms, and the absence of a consent record.

**Efficient cause:** voice actors, comedians, artisans, translators, archivists, animators, festival organisers and creator unions.

**Final cause:** creators control their work; culture is preserved; audiences can trust provenance; and the attention economy pays the people who make the meaning.

**Axiom list:**

1. A voice, a joke and a craft pattern are identifiable creative assets.
2. Consent is not a one-time checkbox; it is a scoped, revocable licence.
3. Provenance increases value when it is verifiable and legible.
4. Cultural symbols carry meaning; outside use requires consultation, not only credit.
5. Preservation is cheaper than reconstruction, but restoration is still expensive.
6. Fan translation is real creative labour; legalising it can expand access.
7. Contracts are the hidden algorithm of the creative economy.
8. Attention is scarce; trust and authenticity are differentiators.

**Derivation:** A1 + A2 → consent-based voice corpus and licensing. A3 → craft provenance and heritage digitisation. A4 → cultural misappropriation check. A5 → local film archive restoration. A6 → fan-translation rights marketplace. A7 → creator contract transparency. A8 → esports integrity, sound library and live acoustics.

## Portfolio Logic

The portfolio protects the **creative commons and the creator's wallet**: consent and rights (rows 1, 6, 7), provenance and culture (rows 4, 9, 11), preservation (rows 5, 3), fair play and credit (rows 2, 8), and craft infrastructure (rows 10, 12). Each row is a small institution that makes creativity legible, licensable and paid.

What is excluded: another AI video generator, another content marketplace, another NFT platform, and another "creator economy" dashboard. Those are crowded or do not change the rights and provenance layer.

## Kill Criteria and Review Notes

- Every row has a measurable creative outcome: consent signed, royalties paid, cheats detected, sounds licensed, artisan income, rights signed, contracts renegotiated, credits awarded, scans completed.
- The hard dependencies are unions and guilds (rows 1, 7, 8), publishers (row 6), archives (row 5), community partners (rows 4, 9, 11), and studios (rows 3, 12).
- The "who pays" answer is the AI lab, studio, publisher, brand, venue, tournament organiser or cultural funder that needs the rights or trust.
- The ethics guardrail: no blanket consent, no cultural extraction, no public shaming without evidence, no fake provenance, and no exploitation of fan labour.
