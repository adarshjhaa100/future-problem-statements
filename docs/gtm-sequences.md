# GTM Sequences — The First 30 Days for Any Row

Each problem row has its own audience, hook, levers, channel, first-100 plan, loop and ethics guardrail. This file turns that into a repeatable 30-day launch sequence. It is not a generic funnel; it is a trust-building sequence for sceptical, busy or low-income users.

The sequence assumes the team has already chosen **one container of trust**: one building, clinic, school, ward, market, factory, feeder, village or fandom. Density beats reach.

## Week 0 — Before any message

1. **Name the binding gap.** Is it awareness, salience, agency, trust or friction? Do not run a campaign until this is written down.
2. **Choose the trusted messenger.** The person the audience already listens to: ASHA, pharmacist, teacher, landlord association secretary, FPO leader, priest, union steward, editor, retired officer.
3. **Build the proof artifact.** One before/after reading, one paid claim, one repaired object, one saved rupee, one avoided hospital visit. A promise is not proof.
4. **Set the ask.** The smallest meaningful action: allow a sensor, take a 10-minute test, attend a 20-minute demo, share a bill, sign a trial consent.
5. **Set the kill metric.** The number that decides whether the pilot continues.

## Week 1 — Trust and reciprocity

The goal is not conversion. The goal is to become a known, useful presence.

- **Give before asking.** Free audit, free test, free sample, free heat alert, free document review, free safety check.
- **Use the trusted messenger to introduce you.** A message from the ASHA worker or the FPO leader outperforms a branded message.
- **Do a public demo.** One table outside the clinic, one stall in the market, one live demo in the workshop, one screening in the school.
- **Collect the baseline.** Measure the problem before the solution: indoor temperature, antibiotic prescriptions, leak rate, claim time, energy bill, eviction risk.
- **Recruit the first 10.** Not 100. Ten people who will tell the truth and let you watch them use it.

**Message shape:** "We are testing [specific fix] with [trusted local partner]. It costs you nothing for the first [period]. We will show you the before/after. If it does not work, we will say so."

## Week 2 — Proof and social proof

- **Show, do not tell.** A side-by-side photo, a bill, a temperature graph, a repaired wheelchair, a paid claim, a court bundle accepted by a clerk.
- **Use a peer, not a brand.** A landlord quotes another landlord; a farmer quotes a farmer; a clinician quotes a clinician.
- **Make the first 10 visible.** A building league table, a shop window sticker, a clinic poster, a village notice board. Descriptive norms ("37 of 42 shops on this street") outperform aspirational claims.
- **Handle the strongest objection in public.** The objection is usually cost, trust, effort, privacy or "it will not work here". Answer it with evidence, not reassurance.
- **Offer a risk reversal.** Refundable deposit, "keep it if it works", pilot price, free replacement, money-back guarantee. Only offer what you can honour.

## Week 3 — The ask and the default

- **Make the desired action the default.** Pre-fill the form, schedule the visit, install the kit before move-in, auto-enrol the savings, book the follow-up.
- **Reduce the steps to one.** One WhatsApp message, one signature, one UPI mandate, one photo. If it needs an app download, expect 70% drop-off.
- **Use a concrete if-then plan.** "When the CO₂ alert goes off, open the two red windows." "When the heat index crosses 40°C, the payout lands in your account."
- **Create honest scarcity.** A pilot cohort of 50, a first-100 clinic programme, a training batch of 20. Never fake it.
- **Ask for a small public commitment.** A signed landlord pledge, a team registration, a shop sticker, a parent meeting RSVP. Small commitments predict follow-through.

## Week 4 — The loop and the proof review

- **Publish the result, including failures.** A one-page before/after with the baseline, the change, the cost and the caveats.
- **Turn users into referrers.** A visible referral card, a WhatsApp forward, a "bring your neighbour" incentive that is useful, not cash-for-votes.
- **Close the loop with the payer.** Show the insurer the claim time, the utility the peak shift, the municipality the avoided cost, the buyer the rejected batch.
- **Decide: continue, narrow or kill.** Use the pre-registered kill metric. Do not move the goalposts.
- **Document the next container.** The second building, clinic, ward or market should be cheaper because the proof and the script exist.

## Audience-specific sequences

### Landlords
- **Week 1:** free heat/energy audit and a peer landlord coffee meeting.
- **Week 2:** show a building-level comparison and the insurance/utility rebate.
- **Week 3:** offer a tenancy-safe kit with a guaranteed rent or no-vacancy clause.
- **Week 4:** publish the building score and recruit the next building.
- **Primary levers:** loss aversion (vacancy, liability), social proof (peer landlord), reciprocity (free audit).
- **Avoid:** environmental sermons, tenant-blaming, or a product that requires structural renovation.

### Clinicians
- **Week 1:** a senior clinician champion and a 15-minute ward huddle.
- **Week 2:** show the baseline and the peer comparison, not a lecture.
- **Week 3:** embed the tool in the existing workflow as the default, with a clear escalation path.
- **Week 4:** share outcome data and ask the champion to present at a professional meeting.
- **Primary levers:** professional identity, peer comparison, liability reduction, workflow default.
- **Avoid:** public shaming, extra clicks, or a tool that creates more work without a clear patient benefit.

### Municipal officials
- **Week 1:** bring a specific, local audit, not a vision deck.
- **Week 2:** show the avoided liability or the citizen demand, with a peer city example.
- **Week 3:** offer a pilot that fits an existing budget line and requires no new procurement category.
- **Week 4:** publish a citizen-facing result that gives the official political cover.
- **Primary levers:** loss aversion (liability, scandal), social proof (peer city), identity (good administrator).
- **Avoid:** public shaming, partisan framing, or a pilot that needs a new law.

### Farmers and fishers
- **Week 1:** a demo plot or landing-site test with the FPO leader.
- **Week 2:** show the before/after yield, loss, income or spoilage, in local language.
- **Week 3:** bundle the product with the buyer's acceptance rule (premium, rejection, payment).
- **Week 4:** recruit through the cooperative and train a local champion.
- **Primary levers:** loss aversion (crop/pond/catch loss), trusted messenger, social proof (neighbour).
- **Avoid:** app-only advice, English charts, or a product that requires upfront cash without a buyer link.

### SMBs and factory owners
- **Week 1:** a free audit with a named peer and a rough ROI.
- **Week 2:** show downtime, penalty or energy cost avoided, not "innovation".
- **Week 3:** offer a paid pilot with a performance guarantee and a clear exit.
- **Week 4:** publish a case study and ask the industry association to circulate it.
- **Primary levers:** loss aversion (downtime), ROI, social proof (peer firm), identity (smart operator).
- **Avoid:** ESG language, long procurement, or a pilot that disrupts production.

### Creators and cultural communities
- **Week 1:** listen, do not extract. Pay for the first workshop or review.
- **Week 2:** show a concrete rights or income win for a respected creator.
- **Week 3:** offer a scoped, revocable licence with transparent terms.
- **Week 4:** publish the first licensed work and share the revenue.
- **Primary levers:** identity, reciprocity, trust, loss aversion (rights grab).
- **Avoid:** blanket buyouts, cultural tokenism, or "exposure" as payment.

### Defence and space partners
- **Week 1:** a compliance and security briefing, not a public demo.
- **Week 2:** a bench test or exercise with the programme office.
- **Week 3:** a narrow paid pilot with a clear security and export-control plan.
- **Week 4:** a readiness or reliability report for the next budget gate.
- **Primary levers:** loss aversion (mission failure), readiness, compliance, sovereignty.
- **Avoid:** public hype, unauthorised data, or any claim you cannot audit.

## Message templates

### WhatsApp / SMS (low-income household)
> [Trusted messenger name] here. We are testing [specific fix] in [place]. It is free for the first [period]. We will check [specific measurement] before and after. Reply YES if you want a visit. No payment now.

### Door-to-door (landlord)
> Your top-floor unit was [X]°C hotter than the ground floor last summer. We can install a tenancy-safe kit and show you the before/after. The kit is free for the pilot, and your insurer may discount the building. Can we measure one unit on [day]?

### B2B email (factory owner)
> Your [machine/feeder/line] had [X] hours of unplanned downtime last quarter. We can install a [sensor/retrofit] on one unit for 30 days and show you the avoided loss. If it does not find a problem, you pay nothing. Are you open to a 20-minute walk-through on [day]?

### Clinician peer message
> Dr [name], we are testing [tool] on [ward] for [condition]. It adds [X] seconds to the workflow and flags [specific risk]. The baseline is [number]. Would you review the protocol before we start?

### Municipal pitch
> This [junction/school/ward] has [specific count] of [crashes/heat days/leaks]. A [cost] retrofit can reduce [outcome]. We will fund the pilot and publish the before/after. We need one engineer and one signature to start.

### Creator / community
> We want to license [work/voice/sound] for [specific use], for [duration], in [territory], with [revocable clause]. The fee is [amount] and you approve the final use. Here is the one-page contract.

## Metrics that matter

- **Activation:** the percentage of the first 100 who complete the smallest meaningful action.
- **Proof:** the measured change against the baseline.
- **Trust:** the percentage who would recommend it to a peer (not "satisfaction").
- **Loop:** the percentage of new users who arrive through a user or partner, not paid acquisition.
- **Payer:** the percentage of the cost covered by an institution that bears the failure.
- **Kill:** whether the pre-registered stop rule was hit.

A campaign that produces high awareness and low activation is a failure. A campaign that produces ten truthful proofs and a payer conversation is a success, even if the reach is small.
