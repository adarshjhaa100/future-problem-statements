# Aggressive but Ethical GTM — A Behavioural-Science Playbook

The marketing column in every problem row is not "run ads". It is a specific adoption design: who must act, what they believe today, what evidence or emotion moves them, which channel reaches them with trust, and what loop makes adoption self-sustaining. This playbook defines the shared vocabulary and the ethical floor.

## 1. The core diagnosis

Most solutions fail at one of five behavioural gaps:

1. **Awareness gap** — the person does not know the problem exists or applies to them.
2. **Salience gap** — they know, but it does not feel urgent today.
3. **Agency gap** — they know and care, but do not believe their action matters or is possible.
4. **Trust gap** — they do not believe the provider, the claim, or the institution.
5. **Friction gap** — they believe and want to act, but the steps are too many, too confusing, or too costly.

The wrong move is to answer every gap with "awareness". A trust gap cannot be solved with a billboard. A friction gap cannot be solved with a sermon. The first GTM decision is **which gap is binding**.

## 2. The behavioural levers

Use no more than three levers per campaign. More than three is usually a sign the audience is not understood.

### Loss aversion
People act to avoid a loss more readily than to capture an equal gain. Use it when the status quo is quietly destructive: heat death, mould, a denied claim, a seized asset, a failed audit. Never invent a loss; make the real one concrete and local.

- Good: "Your top-floor flat was 6.4°C hotter than the ground floor last May. The kit costs less than one month of cooling bills."
- Bad: "Climate change is coming."

### Social proof (descriptive norms)
Show what similar, trusted people actually do — not what they say. "37 of 42 shops on this street switched" beats "everyone is switching".

### Default effect
Make the safe option the pre-set option: auto-enrol, opt-out, pre-filled forms, kits installed before move-in, the healthy choice as the first menu item.

### Friction removal
Reduce steps, documents, waiting, and jargon. A one-click application beats a 12-field form. A WhatsApp flow beats an app download. A local human beats a chatbot for a first-time user.

### Commitment and consistency
Small public commitments predict later behaviour. Use a signed landlord pledge, a visible tenant score, a team registration, a "first 10" list. Keep commitments small enough that people keep them.

### Reciprocity
Give something real before asking: a free sensor, a diagnostic, a legal template, a heat alert, a repair check. Reciprocity is strongest when the gift is useful and not obviously a sales trap.

### Identity and dignity
People adopt products that make them feel like the kind of person they want to be: a good parent, a professional, a steward, a patriot, a craftsperson, a prudent provider. Avoid shame. Shame produces defensiveness and hiding, not behaviour change.

### Trusted messenger
The messenger matters more than the message for low-trust audiences. Use community health workers, local pharmacists, postmasters, teachers, religious leaders, trade associations, union stewards, and peer farmers. A remote brand cannot borrow trust it has not earned.

### Anchoring and price framing
Anchor against the cost of the failure, not the cost of the product: "₹1,200 kit vs ₹40,000 hospital bill" or "one week of wasted diesel vs the sensor". For B2B, anchor against downtime, not against a competitor's licence fee.

### Implementation intentions
Turn intention into a concrete if-then plan: "When the alert says CO₂ > 1,200 ppm, open the two red-marked windows." Implementation intentions outperform motivation alone.

### Fresh start and streaks
Use natural restart points (new school year, new financial year, Monday, move-in day, harvest) to launch habits. Streaks and visible progress help, but do not punish a broken streak; make re-entry easy.

### Scarcity and exclusivity
Use sparingly and honestly: a limited pilot cohort, a first-100 clinic programme, a waiting list. Fake scarcity destroys trust permanently.

### Gain framing for prevention
For preventive products, loss framing can backfire if it feels like fear-mongering. Use gain framing with a concrete near-term benefit: "sleep cooler this week", "fewer sick days this term", "faster claims this month".

## 3. Channel logic by audience

| Audience | First channel | Trusted messenger | What usually fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low-income households | WhatsApp/IVR + local meeting | ASHA, teacher, shopkeeper, postmaster | App-download campaigns, English-only copy |
| Landlords/SME owners | Trade association + peer group | A respected local operator | Generic ESG language |
| Clinicians | Professional body + workflow default | Senior clinician champion | Consumer-style ads |
| Municipal officials | Audit + political cover | Mayor's office, comptroller | Shaming them publicly |
| Farmers | Demo plot + peer farmer | FPO leader, input dealer | Pure app-based advice |
| Defence/procurement | Exercise + compliance file | Programme office, retired officer | Public hype |
| Journalists/creators | Mission + revenue tool | Editor/peer collective | "AI will replace you" |
| Investors/donors | Evidence + risk reduction | Portfolio peer | Moral pressure alone |

## 4. The first 100 users

Every row should have a concrete first-100 plan. The strongest patterns:

- **One container of trust:** one apartment building, one clinic, one school, one factory, one feeder, one market, one village. Density beats reach.
- **A named champion:** one respected person who will demo, vouch, and answer questions.
- **A visible proof artifact:** a before/after reading, a paid claim, a repaired wheelchair, a lower bill, a faster clearance.
- **A low-risk entry:** free audit, refundable deposit, pilot price, "we install, you keep it if it works".
- **A waiting list:** creates social proof and lets the team control quality.

## 5. The growth loop

A campaign is not a loop. A loop is when each adoption makes the next adoption cheaper or more credible.

Examples:
- Heat score → insurer discount → landlord association promotes → more scores.
- Repair network → each repaired wheelchair becomes a visible referral point → more repairs.
- Claim payout video → more policyholders → more data → faster claims.
- Teacher retention alert → one school avoids a resignation → district adopts → more data.
- Local news micro-donation → funded story → visible civic win → more donors.

If there is no loop, the GTM is a subsidy. That can be fine for a pilot, but it must be named.

## 6. Pricing and the "who pays" test

- **Consumer:** price below the monthly cost of the problem, or bundle into an existing bill.
- **B2B:** price as a fraction of avoided downtime, avoided penalty, or new revenue. Offer a paid pilot and a performance guarantee.
- **Government:** sell the audit, the compliance file, or the avoided liability — not "innovation".
- **Insurer:** sell loss-ratio reduction and claims-speed improvement.
- **Platform:** sell retention, trust, or regulatory cover.
- **Cooperative:** sell a service the members already pay for, at lower cost or higher reliability.

If no one will pay, find the institution that already pays for the failure (hospital, insurer, municipality, utility, employer). That is the budget line.

## 7. The ethical floor

Aggressive marketing is not manipulation. The following are prohibited:

- Fabricated statistics, fake testimonials, fake scarcity.
- Fear appeals about a harm that does not exist or is not local.
- Shaming vulnerable people for structural problems.
- Surveillance disguised as care.
- Dark patterns that make cancellation or exit hard.
- Exploiting cognitive biases to sell something the user does not need.
- Using community trust to push a product that the community cannot verify.

The test: **would the intended user, after using the product for six months, endorse the way they were recruited?** If not, redesign.

## 8. The GTM template used in every row

Each row's marketing cell answers seven questions in compressed form:

1. **Who** must act first?
2. **What do they believe today** that blocks action?
3. **What is the one concrete, local proof** that changes the belief?
4. **Which lever** (loss aversion, social proof, default, friction, reciprocity, identity, trusted messenger) is primary?
5. **Which channel** reaches them with trust?
6. **What is the first-100 move?**
7. **What is the loop and the ethical guardrail?**

This is why the GTM column looks different for a landlord, a surgeon, a municipal engineer, and a teenager. The behavioural science is in the diagnosis, not in a universal slogan.
