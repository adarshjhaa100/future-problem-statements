# Research Base — October 2026

This is the shared evidence spine for the 144 problem statements. It is deliberately conservative: where a number is an estimate, a projection, or contested, that is stated. Domain files carry only the facts needed to defend that domain's problems; this file records the cross-domain picture and the sources behind it.

## 1. The 2026 risk picture

The World Economic Forum's *Global Risks Report 2026* (January 2026, survey of 1,300+ leaders and experts) describes an "age of competition". **Geoeconomic confrontation** is the top risk most likely to trigger a material global crisis in 2026 (18% of respondents), followed by **state-based armed conflict** (14%). Over two years, misinformation/disinformation, societal polarisation and extreme weather remain in the top five. Over ten years, environmental risks dominate: extreme weather is the top long-term risk, and half of the top ten risks are environmental. Adverse AI outcomes are among the fastest-rising long-term risks. 57% of respondents expect the next decade to be turbulent or stormy.

Implication for this repository: the highest-leverage problems sit where *geoeconomic fragility*, *climate stress*, *information integrity* and *care/health* intersect. A problem that only makes a rich market 5% more efficient is not enough; it must protect something people cannot easily replace.

## 2. Health and human development

- **Mental health:** WHO's *World Mental Health Today* (2025) and 2026 updates estimate more than 1 billion people live with a mental health condition. Depression and anxiety cost roughly **US$1 trillion a year** in lost productivity. Only **9% of people with major depressive disorder receive minimally adequate treatment** globally; in sub-Saharan Africa the figure is about **2%**. There is roughly **one psychiatrist per million people** in low-income countries. Suicide claimed about **727,000 lives in 2021**.
- **Antimicrobial resistance:** WHO (July 2026) estimates bacterial AMR was associated with **4.7 million deaths in 2021**, with **1.14 million directly attributable**. About **1 in 6 laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections** was resistant in 2023. The Lancet/GBD forecast is **39 million deaths attributable to AMR between 2025 and 2050** on current trends.
- **Air pollution and road injury:** WHO's *World Health Statistics 2026* attributes **6.6 million deaths in 2021** to joint household and ambient air pollution. Road traffic killed about **1.18 million people in 2021**; it remains the leading cause of death for ages 5–29.
- **Obesity and diabetes:** WHO (October 2026) estimates **1.1 billion people live with obesity**; adult obesity more than doubled between 1990 and 2024. The 11th IDF Diabetes Atlas (2026) projects **853 million adults with diabetes by 2050**. A large share of type 2 diabetes is undiagnosed or untreated in low- and middle-income countries.
- **Dementia and care:** Alzheimer's Association 2026 data: about **7 million Americans live with Alzheimer's**, supported by **12 million unpaid caregivers**; US dementia cost is estimated at **US$409 billion in 2026**. Globally, dementia cases are expected to nearly triple by 2050. RAND estimates more than **100 million US adults** provide unpaid care to an adult relative or friend.
- **Microplastics:** 2026 systematic reviews confirm micro- and nanoplastics are detectable in blood, placenta, lung and other tissues, but causal links to clinical disease remain unproven. Public concern is running ahead of the evidence (84% of a German survey believed microplastics worsen pre-existing conditions). The honest problem is therefore *exposure reduction and measurement*, not alarmism.

## 3. Food, water, land and nature

- **Hunger and food insecurity:** SOFI 2026 reports **7.8% of the world undernourished in 2025** and **2.1 billion people experiencing moderate or severe food insecurity**. In Africa, about two-thirds of people cannot afford a healthy diet. The 2026 Global Report on Food Crises says acute hunger has doubled over the past decade and two famines were declared in the previous year; WFP's 2026 outlook counts **318 million people acutely food insecure** in countries where it operates.
- **Food loss and waste:** FAO/UNEP data: about **13.2% of food is lost** between harvest and retail, and a further **19% is wasted** at retail, food service and household level. Food loss and waste generates an estimated **8–10% of global greenhouse-gas emissions**.
- **Soil and land:** FAO's *Status of the World's Soil Resources 2026* finds **58% of regional assessments report deterioration since 2015**, with only about 10% improving. About **40% of global land is degraded**, affecting **3.2 billion people**. UNCCD estimates every dollar invested in restoration returns **US$7–30**.
- **Biodiversity:** WWF's *Living Planet Report 2026* finds a **73% average decline in monitored vertebrate wildlife populations** between 1970 and 2022 (35,803 populations, 5,790 species). Freshwater populations fell **85%**, terrestrial **69%**, marine **59%**. Half of monitored populations are stable or increasing, which is the hope signal: recovery is possible where pressure is removed.
- **Water:** billions still lack safely managed water and sanitation; untreated urban sewage and non-revenue water remain large. The problem is rarely "no water" in aggregate; it is capture, leakage, contamination, allocation and maintenance.

## 4. Energy, materials and the AI build-out

- **Renewables:** IRENA reports **5,149 GW of global renewable capacity at end-2025**, with solar and wind accounting for **96.8% of net additions** in 2025. Renewables supplied about **33.8% of world electricity in 2025**, overtaking coal for the first time in the modern era. The bottleneck has shifted from generation cost to **grids, storage, permitting, and materials**.
- **Data centres and AI:** IEA estimates data centres used about **415 TWh in 2024 (1.5% of global electricity)** and could reach **~945 TWh by 2030**. UN University (2026) estimates **448 TWh in 2025**, with water and carbon footprints comparable to a mid-sized country; AI is about 20% of data-centre energy today, rising toward 40% by 2030. Local water stress and grid interconnection are now the binding constraints in many regions.
- **Critical minerals:** IEA's *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026* warns that export controls have turned concentration risk into an immediate economic-security problem. China dominates refining of rare earths and a large share of gallium, germanium, graphite and battery materials. Up to **US$6.5 trillion a year of downstream production** outside China could be affected by full rare-earth controls; graphite disruption puts **US$300 billion a year** at risk. Planned refining and magnet capacity outside China still lags mine output.
- **Counter-UAS and defence:** the counter-drone market is estimated at **US$14.4 billion in 2026**, growing about 22% a year. The US Congressional Budget Office estimates **US$74 million to equip one military installation** with a layered counter-small-UAS defence and **US$5 million a year** to sustain it. Cheap drones have inverted the cost curve; detection, attribution and low-cost defeat matter more than exquisite interceptors.

## 5. Money, work and the informal economy

- **Informal work:** roughly **8 in 10 workers** in India are informal. India has about **63 million MSMEs**; only about **14% have access to formal credit** (Deloitte, 2026). The MSME credit gap is estimated at **₹25–30 lakh crore** (roughly **US$300–360 billion**) depending on methodology. New-to-credit borrowers are a shrinking share of originations.
- **Global financial inclusion:** account ownership has risen, but usage, resilience and credit access lag. Micro-enterprises remain underserved because cash-flow data is fragmented, collateral is thin, and small-ticket lending is expensive to underwrite.
- **AI and work:** agentic AI is moving from pilots to production faster than governance. 2026 surveys show most large enterprises running at least one agent workflow, with cost overruns and security incidents common. The near-term problem is not mass unemployment; it is **task dislocation, verification, liability and the loss of entry-level apprenticeship work**.
- **Information integrity:** documented deepfake fraud losses reached about **US$3.7 billion** by mid-2026, with estimates of **US$40 billion a year** in synthetic-media-enabled fraud. Deepfakes are now a meaningful share of fraud attempts. The measurement is fragmented, which is itself a problem.

## 6. Education, civic life and trust

- **Education:** UNESCO's 2026 Global Education Monitoring Report counts **273 million children and youth out of school**, the seventh consecutive annual increase; one in six school-age children is excluded, and only two in three complete secondary school. Learning poverty remains far above SDG targets.
- **Local news:** local journalism continues to shrink; research links news deserts to lower civic engagement, more polarisation and weaker accountability. Pew (2026) finds only **34% of Americans say local news outlets are extremely/very important**, down from 44% a year earlier. The crisis is not only audience demand; it is the collapse of the business model for low-cost civic coverage.
- **Institutional trust:** trust is local and relational. Trusted messengers (librarians, fire departments, community health workers, local associations) outperform remote institutions, especially for vulnerable groups.

## 7. What this means for problem selection

The 2026 evidence supports six filters. A problem earns a place in this repository only if it passes all six:

1. **Reach × duration:** it affects many people, or a smaller group for a long time, in a way that compounds (children, soil, infrastructure, trust).
2. **Neglectedness:** the credible global team count is low relative to the burden; "another chatbot for X" fails this test.
3. **Efficient-cause leverage:** a 1–3 person team can change the actual cause (incentive, information, maintenance, verification), not just publish a study.
4. **Legible first version:** a pilot can be shipped in under 12 months with a budget under roughly US$150K, without waiting for a new law or a new molecule.
5. **Who pays:** there is a buyer, insurer, utility, municipality, platform, employer or cooperative whose own interest aligns with adoption.
6. **Falsifiability:** there is a pre-registered kill criterion. If the pilot fails, the team stops.

## 8. Source notes and uncertainty

Numbers are rounded; projections are labelled as projections. Where 2026 data are not yet available, the latest 2024–2025 estimate is used and named. The repository treats all statistics as *decision aids*, not ornaments: every problem row states the specific evidence that would change the decision, and a kill criterion that can be measured in the first version.
