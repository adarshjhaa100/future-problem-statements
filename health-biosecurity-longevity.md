# Domain 4: Health, Biosecurity & Longevity

## Research Summary (October 2026)

- WHO estimates bacterial antimicrobial resistance was associated with **4.7 million deaths in 2021**, with **1.14 million directly attributable**. About **1 in 6 laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections** was resistant in 2023. The Lancet/GBD forecast is **39 million deaths attributable to AMR between 2025 and 2050** on current trends.
- WHO's *World Mental Health Today* and 2026 updates estimate more than **1 billion people** live with a mental health condition; only about **9% of people with major depressive disorder receive minimally adequate treatment**. This domain focuses on the physical-health seams that mental-health and care domains do not cover.
- Road traffic killed about **1.18 million people in 2021** and remains the leading cause of death for ages 5–29. Snakebite, maternal haemorrhage and surgical infections are smaller in headline numbers but catastrophically concentrated in low-resource settings.
- The 11th IDF Diabetes Atlas (2026) projects **853 million adults with diabetes by 2050**. Diabetic foot complications are a leading cause of preventable amputation.
- Oral disease affects billions; surgical site infections, blood-culture contamination and hospital hand hygiene are ordinary, measurable failures that consume antibiotics and beds.
- The 12 problems below are deliberately unglamorous. They are the seams where a cheap measurement, a checklist or a piece of trust infrastructure prevents death, disability or lifelong antibiotic dependence.

## Axioms and Aristotelian Frame

**Material cause:** pathogens, blood, tissue, drugs, vaccines, water, and human anatomy. Biology does not negotiate; delay converts treatable conditions into permanent harm.

**Formal cause:** clinical workflows, prescribing habits, discharge rules, stock allocation, sterilisation protocols, and the separation of diagnosis from treatment.

**Efficient cause:** primary-care doctors, nurses, lab technicians, pharmacists, community health workers, hospital infection-control teams, and district health officers.

**Final cause:** people survive childbirth, snakebite, surgery and infection; children keep their teeth and livers; patients get the right drug at the right time; and clinicians are supported rather than blamed.

**Axiom list:**

1. Time-to-treatment is often the strongest predictor of survival and disability.
2. Clinicians respond to peer comparison, workflow defaults and liability more than to guidelines alone.
3. The point of care is where evidence must arrive; a lab result that returns tomorrow changes little.
4. Most hospital-acquired infections are preventable with hand hygiene, sterile technique and feedback.
5. Stockouts are a visibility and redistribution problem before they are a procurement problem.
6. Vaccines and antivenom fail at the last mile because of cold chain, timing and stock, not because the molecule is unknown.
7. Screening without a treatment pathway creates anxiety without benefit.
8. Measurement must be paired with a named person empowered to act.

**Derivation:** A1 + A3 → move the test or checklist to the point of decision. A2 + A4 → feedback and defaults beat lectures. A5 → forecast and redistribute before buying more. A6 → birth-dose and antivenom kits close the last mile. A7 + A8 → every screening row names the referral and the responsible clinician.

## Portfolio Logic

The portfolio is organised around three clocks. Rows 1, 4, 8 and 11 act in **minutes to hours** (prescribing habit, haemorrhage, birth dose, hand hygiene). Rows 2, 3, 6 and 12 act in **days to weeks** (wound infection, snakebite, ulcer, culture). Rows 5, 7, 9 and 10 act over **months to years** (caries, stockouts, long COVID, menopause).

What is excluded: another symptom-checker chatbot, another telemedicine marketplace, another wearable that measures a biomarker without a treatment pathway, and another "AI doctor". Those are crowded or fail the "who acts on the result" test.

## Kill Criteria and Review Notes

- Every row names the clinical owner and the referral pathway. Screening without treatment is prohibited.
- The hard dependencies are pharmacy data (row 1), clinical governance (rows 2, 6, 9, 10), a toxinology partner (row 3), vaccine cold chain (row 8), and state data access (row 7).
- The "who pays" answer is the hospital, insurer, health department or employer that already bears the cost of the failure.
- The ethics guardrail: no individual surveillance without consent, no public shaming of clinicians, no off-label claims, and no deployment without a red-flag escalation path.
