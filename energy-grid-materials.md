# Domain 2: Energy, Grid & Critical Materials

## Research Summary (October 2026)

- IRENA reports **5,149 GW of renewable capacity at end-2025**, with solar and wind accounting for **96.8% of net additions**. Renewables supplied about **33.8% of world electricity in 2025**, overtaking coal for the first time in the modern era. The bottleneck has moved from generation cost to **grids, storage, permitting and materials**.
- IEA estimates data centres used about **415 TWh in 2024 (1.5% of global electricity)** and could reach **~945 TWh by 2030**. The UN University's 2026 estimate is 448 TWh for 2025, with water and carbon footprints comparable to a mid-sized country. AI is about 20% of data-centre energy today and could approach 40% by 2030.
- IEA's *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026* says export controls have turned concentration risk into an immediate economic-security problem. China dominates refining of rare earths and a large share of gallium, germanium, graphite and battery materials. Up to **US$6.5 trillion a year of downstream production** outside China could be affected by full rare-earth controls; graphite disruption puts **US$300 billion a year** at risk.
- The distribution grid is the least instrumented part of the power system. Utilities know transmission lines and large substations; low-voltage transformers, feeders and phase balance are often invisible until something fails.
- Energy efficiency in MSMEs is a **maintenance and incentives** problem, not a technology problem. Cheap rewinds, unbalanced feeders and unmeasured motors quietly waste 5–15% of industrial electricity for decades.
- The 12 problems below deliberately avoid new solar panels, new batteries and another carbon marketplace. They attack the **actualising conditions** of the transition: monitoring, flexibility, materials recovery, and the last-mile institutions that maintain the grid.

## Axioms and Aristotelian Frame

**Material cause:** electrons, copper, silicon, magnets, heat, gas, and the physical age of assets. Energy is conserved; conversion loses quality; materials are finite and geographically concentrated.

**Formal cause:** tariffs, interconnection rules, asset ownership, procurement, and the split between capital budgets (new assets) and maintenance budgets (keeping old assets alive).

**Efficient cause:** distribution engineers, transformer repair shops, data-centre operators, MSME owners, rewinders, city-gas safety officers, and small teams who can instrument the unmeasured.

**Final cause:** reliable, affordable, clean power; safe cooking; a circular materials base; and a transition that does not sacrifice the poor to grid instability.

**Axiom list:**

1. Energy conversion always loses useful work; waste heat and losses are physical, not moral failures.
2. Generation and demand must balance in real time; flexibility has value even when it produces nothing.
3. The distribution grid is where most outages, losses and fires happen, and it is the least measured.
4. Critical minerals are concentrated; recycling is a security strategy, not only an environmental one.
5. Capital budgets favour new assets; maintenance budgets are politically weak, so old assets fail silently.
6. MSMEs respond to cash flow and downtime, not to ESG language.
7. Data centres are both a load problem and a flexibility resource; the difference is scheduling and incentives.
8. Safety devices that only warn do not close the loop; actuation and rebooking do.

**Derivation:** A2 + A3 → the cheapest megawatt is often a better-managed distribution grid. A4 → short-loop recycling reduces geopolitical exposure. A5 → monitoring and maintenance products must be cheap enough to fit opex. A6 → sell uptime and bills, not sustainability. A8 → connect the sensor to the valve, the alert to the rebooking.

## Portfolio Logic

The portfolio has three layers. Rows 1, 2, 6, 9 and 12 make the **distribution grid visible and controllable** — the cheapest capacity is often the capacity you stop wasting or losing. Rows 3 and 4 turn data centres from a pure load into a **flexibility and heat resource**. Rows 5, 7, 8 and 10 attack the **materials and access** layer: efficiency, recycling, and renters who cannot put panels on a roof. Row 11 is the life-safety outlier, included because cooking gas remains one of the largest preventable household killers.

What is excluded: another solar marketplace, another battery-management system, another carbon-credit platform, and generic "smart grid" dashboards. Those are saturated or fail the maintenance-payer test.

## Kill Criteria and Review Notes

- Every row has a measured first-version result; most are 8–12 month pilots, not 3-month demos.
- The hard dependencies are utility data access, PESO certification (row 11), a smelter or magnet partner (rows 7–8), and a lab (row 9). No partner, no start.
- The economics must be expressed in avoided failure, avoided loss, or paid flexibility. "Efficiency" alone is not a budget line.
- Safety rows (11, 12) must be validated with the authority, not only with users. A cheap device that fails once can kill the category.
- The ethical guardrail: no surveillance of workers or households; utility and customer data stay with their owners; no greenwashing of recovered materials.
