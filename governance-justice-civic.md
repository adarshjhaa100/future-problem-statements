# Domain 11: Governance, Justice & Civic Infrastructure

## Research Summary (October 2026)

- Local journalism continues to shrink. Pew's 2026 local news fact sheet finds only **34% of Americans say local news outlets are extremely or very important**, down from 44% a year earlier. Research links news deserts to lower civic engagement, more polarisation and weaker accountability.
- Trust is local and relational. Trusted messengers — librarians, community health workers, local associations, courts — outperform remote institutions, especially for vulnerable groups.
- Courts and administrative systems are document-heavy and opaque. Self-represented litigants, limited legal aid, interpreter shortages and delayed complaints turn ordinary problems into years of insecurity.
- The 12 problems below are **civic infrastructure**: the small protocols, trackers and navigation layers that make rights, meetings, complaints and benefits usable. They are not another "govtech dashboard".

## Axioms and Aristotelian Frame

**Material cause:** documents, meetings, testimony, evidence, languages, time, and the attention of overstretched officials.

**Formal cause:** court rules, freedom-of-information law, municipal procedures, eligibility rules, and the absence of a tracking number.

**Efficient cause:** court clerks, legal aid lawyers, interpreters, journalists, municipal staff, police complaint cells, and community organisations.

**Final cause:** a resident can see what their council decided; a litigant can tell their story in order; a family can visit a prisoner; a survivor can get relief; and a complaint is answered.

**Axiom list:**

1. A right without a usable process is not a right in practice.
2. Transparency requires capture, structure and a public artifact, not only a meeting.
3. Legal outcomes depend on evidence organisation and language access.
4. Complaints disappear without a tracking number and an escalation path.
5. Benefits are claimed by those who can navigate complexity; navigation is a redistributive act.
6. Family connection reduces recidivism and is cheaper than reoffending.
7. Restorative processes require consent, safety and trained facilitators.
8. Missing-person and disaster responses are time-critical coordination problems.

**Derivation:** A1 → legal aid matching, interpreter scheduling, evidence organiser. A2 → municipal meeting transparency, contract change-order tracking, FOI assistant. A3 → court evidence and interpreter. A4 → police complaint tracking. A5 → disaster relief navigator. A6 → prison family connection. A7 → restorative justice.

## Portfolio Logic

The portfolio is **civic plumbing**: information (rows 1, 2, 7, 8), rights (rows 5, 6, 12), accountability (rows 4, 9), family and time-critical response (rows 3, 10, 11). Each row produces a public artifact — a funded story, a meeting record, a tracking number, a court bundle — that makes the system legible.

What is excluded: another e-government portal, another generic legal chatbot, another "civic engagement" app, and another transparency dashboard without a user. Those are crowded or fail the "who uses it on Monday" test.

## Kill Criteria and Review Notes

- Every row has a measurable civic outcome: donations, meetings covered, visits, cases resolved, requests answered, complaints tracked, benefits received.
- The hard dependencies are courts (rows 4, 5, 6, 12), municipal councils (rows 2, 7), police/prisons (rows 3, 9, 10), and disaster agencies (row 11).
- The "who pays" answer is the newsroom, court, municipality, foundation or agency that bears the cost of the failure.
- The ethics guardrail: no surveillance, no doxxing, no unauthorised legal practice, no manipulation of procurement data, no exploitation of victims or families, and no claim of neutrality without disclosure.
