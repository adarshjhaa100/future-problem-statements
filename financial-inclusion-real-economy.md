# Domain 8: Financial Inclusion & the Real Economy

## Research Summary (October 2026)

- India has about **63 million MSMEs**; only about **14% have access to formal credit** (Deloitte, 2026). The MSME credit gap is estimated at **₹25–30 lakh crore** (roughly **US$300–360 billion**) depending on methodology.
- Roughly **8 in 10 workers** in India are informal. Account ownership has risen, but usage, resilience and credit access lag. Micro-enterprises remain underserved because cash-flow data is fragmented, collateral is thin, and small-ticket lending is expensive to underwrite.
- New-to-credit borrowers are a shrinking share of originations. The people who most need a first loan are the least likely to get one.
- The problems below are not another UPI app or another lending marketplace. They are the **missing contracts, registries and trust mechanisms** that make small real-economy finance possible: movable collateral, trade-credit insurance, rent-to-own, cooperative procurement, honest claims and enforceable payment terms.

## Axioms and Aristotelian Frame

**Material cause:** cash flow, assets, inventory, livestock, invoices, and the timing mismatch between income and obligations.

**Formal cause:** collateral law, credit bureaus, insurance contracts, payment terms, cooperative rules, and the lack of a verifiable lien.

**Efficient cause:** equipment dealers, anchor buyers, cooperatives, insurers, warehouse operators, lenders, and community finance workers.

**Final cause:** a worker can own the machine that earns their living; a supplier is paid on time; a family is not destroyed by a funeral; a farmer can sell when the price is fair; and a thin-file borrower gets a second chance.

**Axiom list:**

1. A verifiable receivable or asset is collateral; an unverifiable one is not.
2. Small-ticket finance is expensive because fixed underwriting costs are spread over tiny amounts.
3. Daily or weekly repayment matches informal cash flow better than monthly.
4. Insurance is sold by trust and retained by fast, honest claims.
5. Delayed payment is a transfer of working capital from the weak to the strong.
6. Aggregation gives micro-retailers bargaining power they cannot get alone.
7. Commitment devices help people save when income is irregular.
8. A credit score is a story about the future; it can be repaired if the underlying behaviour changes.

**Derivation:** A1 → movable collateral registry and invoice discounting. A2 + A3 → nano-loans and rent-to-own with flexible repayment. A4 → funeral and livestock insurance with fast claims. A5 → delayed-payment enforcement. A6 → cooperative procurement. A7 → savings commitment. A8 → credit repair.

## Portfolio Logic

The portfolio addresses four finance gaps: **asset ownership** (rows 1, 7, 10, 11), **working capital** (rows 2, 5, 12), **resilience** (rows 3, 4, 9), and **market power** (rows 6, 8). Each row turns something real — a machine, an invoice, a cow, a grain bag, a repayment habit — into a verifiable financial object.

What is excluded: another UPI app, another lending marketplace, another generic credit score, and another micro-insurance aggregator. Those are crowded or do not fix the underlying collateral or trust problem.

## Kill Criteria and Review Notes

- Every row has a measurable financial outcome: default, interest burden, claim time, recovery, savings, discount rate.
- The hard dependencies are lender/insurer partners (rows 1, 2, 4, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12), legal recognition (rows 5, 7), and cooperative governance (row 6).
- The "who pays" answer is the borrower's future cash flow, the buyer's balance sheet, the insurer's loss ratio, or the lender's new asset. If none is true, the product is a subsidy.
- The ethics guardrail: transparent total cost, no predatory repossession, no fake credit repair, no exploitation of grief, no hidden insurance exclusions, and no debt product that worsens the trap.
