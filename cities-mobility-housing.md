# Domain 9: Cities, Mobility & Housing

## Research Summary (October 2026)

- WHO's *World Health Statistics 2026* estimates about **1.18 million road traffic deaths in 2021**; road injury remains the leading cause of death for ages 5–29. More than 90% of road deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries.
- Urban heat, air pollution and inadequate housing are not separate crises. A top-floor rental, a bus stop without shade, a school gate on a highway and a construction site in a heat wave are the same failure: **the people who bear the risk do not control the design**.
- Cities are full of underused assets: vacant homes, empty loading bays, unallocated vending space, and sidewalks that exclude wheelchair users. The problem is often allocation and maintenance, not construction.
- Informal workers — street vendors, waste pickers, night-shift staff, construction labourers — keep cities running while bearing the worst conditions. A small protocol can improve safety and income without a mega-project.
- The 12 problems below are deliberately **street-level**. They can be piloted in one ward, one market, one school or one junction, and they produce visible proof within a season.

## Axioms and Aristotelian Frame

**Material cause:** asphalt, shade, housing stock, vehicles, footpaths, heat, and the human body in motion.

**Formal cause:** property rights, zoning, permits, street design standards, lease terms, and the split between who owns an asset and who bears the risk.

**Efficient cause:** municipal engineers, traffic police, landlords, vendors' associations, transport operators, construction contractors, and waste pickers.

**Final cause:** a city where a child can walk to school, a wheelchair can reach the bus, a vendor has a legal spot, a renter is not evicted into homelessness, and a night-shift worker gets home safely.

**Axiom list:**

1. Crashes, heat and evictions cluster at specific places; place-based fixes beat generic policy.
2. Public space is allocateable; the absence of allocation creates conflict and corruption.
3. Shade, lighting, benches and footpaths are health infrastructure.
4. Split incentives reproduce inefficiency: the owner of the shell does not pay the energy bill.
5. Informal workers are the operating system of the city; excluding them raises costs for everyone.
6. Vacant assets are a failure of trust and finance, not only of supply.
7. A safe route is a sequence of small design decisions, not one big project.
8. Maintenance is the actualising condition of every urban improvement.

**Derivation:** A1 → black-spot retrofit and school-zone audits. A2 → vending allocation and loading-bay booking. A3 → bus-stop shade and sidewalk audits. A4 → tenant efficiency fix. A5 → waste-picker integration. A6 → vacant housing activation. A7 → night transport safety.

## Portfolio Logic

The portfolio is a **street-level operating system**: allocate space (rows 2, 10), make movement safe (rows 3, 4, 5, 6, 11), keep people housed (rows 1, 7), protect workers (rows 8, 9), and fix the tenant-landlord energy split (row 12). Each row can start in one ward or one corridor and produce a visible result within one season.

What is excluded: another smart-city dashboard, another ride-hailing app, another generic "15-minute city" plan, and another homelessness app. Those are crowded or do not change the allocation and maintenance incentives.

## Kill Criteria and Review Notes

- Every row has a measurable place-based outcome: crashes, speeds, evictions, heat illness, income, dwell time, accessibility, energy.
- The hard dependencies are municipal partners (rows 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 11), employers (row 6), contractors (row 8), and utilities (row 12).
- The "who pays" answer is the city's avoided liability, the employer's retention, the utility's efficiency programme, or the landlord's asset value.
- The ethics guardrail: no displacement, no victim blaming, no surveillance beyond the public space, no exclusion of women or informal workers, and no safety protocol that exists only on paper.
