# Domain 7: Education, Skills & Future of Work

## Research Summary (October 2026)

- UNESCO's 2026 Global Education Monitoring Report counts **273 million children and youth out of school**, the seventh consecutive annual increase. One in six school-age children is excluded, and only two in three complete secondary school.
- Learning poverty remains far above the SDG target. The problem is not only access; it is **foundational skills, teacher stability, safety, and the transition from learning to earning**.
- AI is changing the entry-level labour market faster than curricula. The near-term risk is not mass unemployment but **the disappearance of apprenticeship tasks** that used to turn education into capability.
- Informal work remains the default in much of the world. Skills are real but unrecognised; credentials are fragmented; hiring runs on networks and trust.
- The 12 problems below focus on **transitions**: school to work, informal to formal, career break to re-entry, disability to accommodation, and learning to a verified credential. They avoid another generic LMS or AI tutor.

## Axioms and Aristotelian Frame

**Material cause:** time, attention, practice, teachers, classrooms, transport, and the physical safety of children.

**Formal cause:** curricula, assessment rules, credential recognition, hiring filters, school funding, and the separation of training from work.

**Efficient cause:** teachers, principals, trade masters, employers, sector councils, alumni, and parents.

**Final cause:** children learn and are safe; young people can demonstrate what they can do; workers can move; women can return; and experience is recognised without a bureaucratic maze.

**Axiom list:**

1. Skills are built through practice with feedback, not through content consumption.
2. Credentials matter only when employers recognise them.
3. Informal apprenticeship is a real educational system; it lacks matching and recognition.
4. AI makes output cheap and process evidence more valuable.
5. Teacher attrition is predictable from workload, isolation and early warning signals.
6. School safety is a route and infrastructure problem, not only a classroom problem.
7. Learning differences are often visible before diagnosis; accommodations can start earlier.
8. Networks reproduce inequality unless they are deliberately opened.

**Derivation:** A1 + A3 → apprenticeship matching with a real master and a trial. A2 → employer-recognised micro-credentials. A4 → process portfolio and live assessment. A5 → teacher retention early warning. A6 → school transport and infrastructure audits. A7 → teacher-administered screening. A8 → first-gen sponsorship.

## Portfolio Logic

The portfolio covers the **transition points** where education fails: matching to a master (row 1), proving gig skill (row 2), safe school infrastructure and transport (rows 3, 10), re-entry (row 4), workplace language (row 5), proof of human skill in an AI market (row 6), teacher stability (row 7), MSME capability (row 8), recognition of informal experience (row 9), early learning differences (row 11), and networks (row 12).

What is excluded: another AI tutor, another LMS, another coding bootcamp, another generic mentoring app, and another "future of work" report. Those are crowded or do not change the efficient cause.

## Kill Criteria and Review Notes

- Every row has a measurable transition outcome: retention, placement, wage, safety, recognition, graduation.
- The hard dependencies are employer partners (rows 2, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12), school/district partners (rows 3, 7, 10, 11), and a master/industry network (row 1).
- The "who pays" answer is the employer, university, school district or sector council that gains from the transition.
- The ethics guardrail: no fees to poor youth, no fake credentials, no surveillance of teachers or students, no labelling of children without consent, and no unpaid labour disguised as training.
